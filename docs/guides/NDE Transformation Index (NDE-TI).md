# **NDE Transformation Index (NDE-TI)**

## **A Narrative-Analysis Scale for Measuring Post-NDE Transformation from First-Person Accounts**

---

## **PART I: LITERATURE FOUNDATION & RATIONALE**

### **1.1 The Transformation Phenomenon**

Near-death experiences are not merely events that happen *to* people—they are events that fundamentally *change* people. This is one of the most robust and consistently replicated findings across five decades of NDE research. Kenneth Ring, in *Heading Toward Omega* (1984), was among the first to systematically document these changes, identifying what he called the "omega prototype"—a pattern of deep, lasting transformation encompassing values, beliefs, personality, and even purported psychic faculties. Ring found that NDErs consistently reported decreased materialism, increased compassion, heightened spiritual awareness, reduced fear of death, and the emergence of psychic or intuitive abilities.

Bruce Greyson formalized the measurement of NDE aftereffects through the Life Changes Inventory (LCI), later revised as the LCI-R (Greyson, 2004), a 50-item self-report instrument that captures changes across nine value domains. Greyson's research, spanning decades at the University of Virginia's Division of Perceptual Studies, demonstrated that these changes are not transient emotional responses but enduring personality-level shifts that deepen over time—a finding corroborated by Pim van Lommel's landmark prospective study published in *The Lancet* (2001), which showed that transformation effects were more pronounced at the 8-year follow-up than at the 2-year follow-up.

P.M.H. Atwater expanded the research lens to include physiological aftereffects—electromagnetic sensitivity, changes in medication tolerance, altered sensory perception—and conducted pioneering research on childhood NDEs, documenting distinct developmental impacts. Cherie Sutherland's Australian research (*Transformed by the Light*, 1992\) confirmed the cross-cultural consistency of these patterns. Jeffrey Long's NDERF database, comprising thousands of accounts with structured survey responses, provided the largest quantitative dataset on aftereffects, while Raymond Moody's and Elisabeth Kübler-Ross's foundational work established the experiential framework.

### **1.2 The Gap This Scale Addresses**

Existing instruments (the Greyson NDE Scale, the LCI-R, Ring's Psychic Experiences Inventory) require the experiencer to complete a structured questionnaire. They cannot be applied retrospectively to narrative accounts—the thousands of interviews, testimonies, books, social media posts, and video transcripts that constitute the vast majority of available NDE data.

The NDE Transformation Index (NDE-TI) is designed specifically for narrative analysis: it enables a trained reader or an AI system to evaluate the transformation described in a first-person account without requiring any additional investigation, survey completion, or follow-up contact with the experiencer. It extracts transformation evidence from what the experiencer chose to share, recognizing that this represents what was most salient and meaningful to them.

## **PART II: SCALE OVERVIEW**

### **2.1 Structure**

The NDE-TI consists of 10 Transformation Domains, each scored on a 0–5 intensity scale, with a direction indicator where applicable. The scale also generates three meta-scores and optional qualitative context notes.

### **2.2 Design Principles**

1. Text-Derivable: Every element can be assessed from a first-person narrative without additional investigation.  
2. Direction-Sensitive: Captures not just *that* change occurred, but *which way* it went.  
3. Neutral on Valence: Does not assume transformation is positive or negative—captures what the experiencer reports.  
4. Breadth and Depth: Distinguishes between wide-ranging transformation across many life areas and deep transformation in a single area.  
5. Academically Grounded: Each domain maps to established constructs in the peer-reviewed NDE literature.  
6. Intuitively Accessible: Described in language a high school student can understand.

### **2.3 What the Scale Measures**

The NDE-TI measures the transformation that an experiencer describes as resulting from their NDE, as expressed in their own account. It does not measure the depth or type of the NDE itself (that is the role of the Greyson NDE Scale). It does not verify whether changes actually occurred—it assesses what the experiencer reports.

### **2.4 Scoring Overview**

* Each Domain: 0–5 (intensity of transformation described)  
* Direction per Domain: ↑ Increased, ↓ Decreased, ↕ Mixed/Complex, → Shifted (e.g., from organized religion to personal spirituality)  
* Overall Transformation Score: Sum of all domain scores (0–50)  
* Transformation Breadth: Count of domains scoring ≥1 (0–10)  
* Transformation Depth: Mean score of domains scoring ≥1 (0.0–5.0)

## **PART III: THE 10 TRANSFORMATION DOMAINS**

---

### **DOMAIN 1: APPRECIATION FOR LIFE (AL)**

Definition: Changes in how much the experiencer values being alive, notices beauty, savors ordinary moments, and feels gratitude for existence.

Literature Basis: Consistently identified as one of the strongest and most universal aftereffects (Ring, 1984; Greyson LCI-R "Appreciation for Life" factor; van Lommel, 2010; Long/NDERF surveys). NDErs frequently describe the world as more vivid, beautiful, and precious after their experience.

What to Look For in the Account:

* Statements about finding beauty in ordinary things (nature, sunlight, children's laughter)  
* Expressions of gratitude for being alive  
* Descriptions of not taking life for granted  
* References to savoring moments, being more present  
* Contrast between taking life for granted before vs. cherishing it after  
* Descriptions of heightened sensory awareness or aesthetic sensitivity  
* Statements about every day being a gift

Scoring Rubric:

| Score | Label | Description |
| ----- | ----- | ----- |
| 0 | Not Addressed | The account does not discuss changes in life appreciation |
| 1 | Briefly Noted | A passing mention of appreciating life more (e.g., "I'm more grateful now") |
| 2 | Mild Change | Some description of increased appreciation with limited detail |
| 3 | Moderate Change | Clear description of a meaningful shift in how life is valued, with examples |
| 4 | Significant Change | Detailed account of transformed relationship with life; described as a major change |
| 5 | Profound Transformation | Life appreciation described as fundamentally, dramatically altered; vivid descriptions; clearly central to their post-NDE identity |

Typical Direction: ↑ (Increase)

Example Statements by Score Level:

* Score 1: *"I appreciate life more now."*  
* Score 3: *"I never noticed how beautiful a sunset was before. Now I can sit and watch one and cry. Everything is so precious to me."*  
* Score 5: *"Every single morning I wake up and I am in awe that I'm here. I see colors I never noticed before. I can spend an hour watching a butterfly. My whole relationship with being alive has completely transformed. Nothing is ordinary to me anymore."*

---

### **DOMAIN 2: SELF-PERCEPTION & IDENTITY (SI)**

Definition: Changes in how the experiencer sees themselves—their self-worth, self-acceptance, inner peace, confidence, personality traits, and sense of who they are.

Literature Basis: Ring (1984) documented increased self-acceptance and inner peace. Greyson's LCI-R includes a "Self-Acceptance" factor. Atwater (2007) noted personality changes including increased assertiveness and emotional sensitivity. Van Lommel (2010) found lasting increases in self-understanding. Many experiencers report feeling like a "different person" after their NDE.

What to Look For in the Account:

* Statements about self-acceptance or self-love  
* Reports of increased or decreased confidence  
* Descriptions of feeling like a different person  
* References to inner peace, calm, or equanimity  
* Changes in patience, temper, or emotional reactivity  
* Statements about no longer needing others' approval  
* Identity shifts (feeling disconnected from who they were before)  
* Increased emotional sensitivity or depth  
* Greater sense of personal worth  
* Struggles with a changed sense of self

Scoring Rubric:

| Score | Label | Description |
| ----- | ----- | ----- |
| 0 | Not Addressed | No mention of changes in self-perception or personality |
| 1 | Briefly Noted | A passing reference to feeling different or seeing themselves differently |
| 2 | Mild Change | Some description of personality or self-concept changes |
| 3 | Moderate Change | Clear description of meaningful identity or personality shifts, with examples |
| 4 | Significant Change | Detailed account of fundamentally changed self-concept; described as major |
| 5 | Profound Transformation | Describes feeling like an entirely different person; deep, detailed exploration of identity transformation |

Typical Direction: ↑ Self-acceptance and inner peace increase; personality shifts; identity may be disrupted before being reconstructed

Example Statements by Score Level:

* Score 1: *"I'm more at peace with myself now."*  
* Score 3: *"I used to be so insecure and constantly worried about what people thought of me. After my NDE, I just accepted myself. I stopped trying to be someone I wasn't."*  
* Score 5: *"I don't recognize the person I was before. My entire personality changed. I was aggressive, impatient, angry—and I came back gentle, patient, with this deep inner stillness I never had. My own family said I was a completely different person. It took years for me to integrate who I was with who I became."*

---

### **DOMAIN 3: COMPASSION & CONCERN FOR OTHERS (CC)**

Definition: Changes in empathy, care for others, desire to help or serve, tolerance, and the capacity for unconditional love.

Literature Basis: Among the most consistently reported aftereffects across all studies (Ring, 1984; Greyson LCI-R "Concern for Others" factor; van Lommel, 2010; Sutherland, 1992; Long/NDERF). NDErs frequently report heightened empathy—sometimes to the point of feeling others' emotions or pain—and an overwhelming desire to be of service. Many describe learning during their NDE that love and how we treat others is what matters most.

What to Look For in the Account:

* Increased empathy or emotional sensitivity to others  
* Desire to help, serve, volunteer, or care for others  
* Greater tolerance or acceptance of different people, beliefs, cultures  
* Descriptions of unconditional love or loving without judgment  
* Reports of feeling others' pain or emotions (empathic sensitivity)  
* Interest in humanitarian or social justice causes  
* Environmental or planetary concern  
* Contrasts between being self-centered before vs. other-centered after  
* Volunteer work, charitable giving, or helping professions

Scoring Rubric:

| Score | Label | Description |
| ----- | ----- | ----- |
| 0 | Not Addressed | No mention of changes in empathy, compassion, or concern for others |
| 1 | Briefly Noted | A passing mention of caring more about others |
| 2 | Mild Change | Some description of increased empathy or desire to help |
| 3 | Moderate Change | Clear description of meaningful growth in compassion, with examples |
| 4 | Significant Change | Detailed account of transformed relationship with others; empathy or service described as a driving force in life |
| 5 | Profound Transformation | Compassion described as the central organizing principle of post-NDE life; vivid descriptions of empathic abilities; dramatic shift from self-focus to other-focus |

Typical Direction: ↑ (Increase)

---

### **DOMAIN 4: VALUES & PRIORITIES (VP)**

Definition: Changes in what the experiencer considers important in life—particularly shifts regarding materialism, status, competition, wealth, social approval, simplicity, and authenticity.

Literature Basis: Decreased materialism and decreased concern with social status are among the most replicated findings in NDE research (Ring, 1984; Greyson LCI-R "Materialism" and "Concern with Impressing Others" factors; van Lommel, 2010; Sutherland, 1992). NDErs consistently report that possessions, money, career prestige, and social image became far less important after their experience, replaced by valuing relationships, love, knowledge, and authentic living.

What to Look For in the Account:

* Statements about money or possessions mattering less  
* Reduced interest in career advancement for status or wealth  
* Decreased competitiveness  
* Less concern with what others think  
* Greater interest in simplicity or minimalism  
* Shift from achievement-orientation to relationship-orientation  
* Changed spending habits or lifestyle  
* Statements about what "really matters" in life  
* Choosing meaning over money in life decisions  
* Giving away possessions or simplifying life

Scoring Rubric:

| Score | Label | Description |
| ----- | ----- | ----- |
| 0 | Not Addressed | No mention of changed values or priorities |
| 1 | Briefly Noted | A passing mention that priorities shifted |
| 2 | Mild Change | Some description of changing what matters |
| 3 | Moderate Change | Clear description of a meaningful reprioritization, with examples |
| 4 | Significant Change | Detailed account of fundamentally reorganized priorities; described as a major life restructuring |
| 5 | Profound Transformation | Entire value system described as overturned; dramatic, concrete examples of life restructured around new values |

Typical Direction: ↓ Materialism, status-seeking, competitiveness; ↑ Simplicity, authenticity, love-centered values

---

### **DOMAIN 5: SPIRITUAL AWARENESS (SA)**

Definition: Changes in the experiencer's sense of connection to something greater than themselves—universal consciousness, the divine, a higher power, oneness, transcendence—and in spiritual practices such as meditation, prayer, contemplation, or energy work.

Literature Basis: Increased spirituality is one of the most prominent and well-documented aftereffects (Ring, 1984; Greyson LCI-R "Spirituality" factor; Khanna & Greyson, 2014; van Lommel, 2010; Long/NDERF). Critically, the research distinguishes between *spirituality* (personal sense of connection to the transcendent) and *religiousness* (adherence to organized religious doctrine and practice). NDErs overwhelmingly report increased spirituality, often becoming more universalist, ecumenical, or mystical in orientation. Many develop new spiritual practices or deepen existing ones.

What to Look For in the Account:

* Descriptions of feeling connected to God, source, universal consciousness, or all that is  
* Sense of oneness or interconnectedness with all life  
* New or deepened spiritual practices (meditation, prayer, contemplation, energy work)  
* Interest in mystical or contemplative traditions  
* Describing themselves as "spiritual" or "more spiritual"  
* Feeling guided or watched over  
* Sense of the sacred in everyday life  
* Interest in spiritual topics, books, teachers  
* Descriptions of inner knowing or direct spiritual experience  
* Universal or non-denominational spiritual orientation

Scoring Rubric:

| Score | Label | Description |
| ----- | ----- | ----- |
| 0 | Not Addressed | No mention of spiritual changes |
| 1 | Briefly Noted | A passing mention of feeling more spiritual |
| 2 | Mild Change | Some description of increased spiritual awareness |
| 3 | Moderate Change | Clear description of meaningful spiritual deepening, with examples |
| 4 | Significant Change | Detailed account of spiritual awakening; spirituality described as a central life change |
| 5 | Profound Transformation | Spirituality described as the defining feature of post-NDE life; vivid descriptions of ongoing mystical or transcendent awareness; complete spiritual reorientation |

Typical Direction: ↑ (Increase). Rare exceptions exist (some distressing NDErs may initially retreat from spirituality before eventually engaging).

---

### **DOMAIN 6: RELIGIOUS ORIENTATION (RO)**

Definition: Changes in the experiencer's relationship with organized religion—including adherence to specific doctrines, participation in religious institutions, identification with a particular faith tradition, and beliefs about religious exclusivism or universalism.

Literature Basis: This is one of the most nuanced domains in NDE research. Greyson's LCI-R includes a "Religiousness" factor, and research consistently shows a complex pattern: NDErs may become *more* religious, *less* religious, or—most commonly—shift *from organized religion toward personal spirituality* (Ring, 1984; van Lommel, 2010; Long/NDERF). Some who were atheist or agnostic develop religious or spiritual beliefs. Some who were devoutly religious become less attached to doctrine while deepening in personal spiritual experience. Some retain their religious framework but reinterpret it. The direction of this change is highly variable and extremely important for users filtering accounts.

What to Look For in the Account:

* Statements about leaving or joining a religion  
* Changes in church/temple/mosque attendance  
* Shifts in doctrinal beliefs (heaven, hell, salvation, reincarnation)  
* Moving from exclusivist ("only my religion is true") to universalist ("all paths lead to God")  
* Greater or lesser interest in scripture, religious study, or theology  
* Conflict with religious community or leaders over NDE  
* Finding religious frameworks inadequate to describe their experience  
* Deepening within existing faith tradition  
* Adopting new religious or philosophical framework  
* Statements about religion vs. spirituality

Scoring Rubric:

| Score | Label | Description |
| ----- | ----- | ----- |
| 0 | Not Addressed | No mention of changes in religious orientation |
| 1 | Briefly Noted | A passing mention of religious change |
| 2 | Mild Change | Some description of shifts in religious beliefs or practice |
| 3 | Moderate Change | Clear description of meaningful religious reorientation, with examples |
| 4 | Significant Change | Detailed account of major change in religious life; significant doctrinal or institutional shift |
| 5 | Profound Transformation | Complete religious reorientation described (e.g., devout to non-religious, atheist to deeply faithful, exclusive to universalist); central to post-NDE narrative |

Direction Indicator (Critical for this domain):

* ↑ Became more religious / deeper in faith tradition  
* ↓ Became less religious / left organized religion  
* → Shifted from religion to spirituality (or from one tradition to another)  
* ↕ Mixed/Complex relationship with religion  
* ✦ Was non-religious, developed spiritual/religious beliefs

---

### **DOMAIN 7: ATTITUDE TOWARD DEATH (AD)**

Definition: Changes in the experiencer's fear of death, understanding of what death is, belief in an afterlife or continuation of consciousness, and overall peace with mortality.

Literature Basis: Reduced fear of death is arguably the single most consistent aftereffect of NDEs, reported across virtually every study (Moody, 1975; Ring, 1980, 1984; Greyson, 1992, 2021; van Lommel, 2001, 2010; Sutherland, 1992; Long/NDERF). NDErs overwhelmingly describe death as a transition rather than an ending, often reporting complete elimination of death anxiety. Many describe death as "going home." This change extends to reduced fear of the dying process and increased comfort being with dying people. Notably, some experiencers of distressing NDEs may initially have *increased* fear before processing leads to reduced fear.

What to Look For in the Account:

* Statements about no longer fearing death  
* Descriptions of death as a transition, homecoming, or doorway  
* Certainty about afterlife or continuation of consciousness  
* Comfort being with dying people or discussing death  
* Loss of fear of the dying process  
* Descriptions of the "other side" as more real or more home-like  
* Statements about not wanting to come back  
* Grief about returning to physical life  
* Changed funeral preferences or death-related decisions  
* Ability to comfort the bereaved

Scoring Rubric:

| Score | Label | Description |
| ----- | ----- | ----- |
| 0 | Not Addressed | No mention of changed attitudes toward death |
| 1 | Briefly Noted | A passing mention of reduced fear or changed beliefs about death |
| 2 | Mild Change | Some description of altered death perspective |
| 3 | Moderate Change | Clear description of meaningfully changed relationship with death/mortality |
| 4 | Significant Change | Detailed account of fundamentally transformed death perspective; described as a major life change |
| 5 | Profound Transformation | Complete elimination of death fear described; death reframed entirely as positive transition; described with absolute certainty and vivid detail |

Typical Direction: ↓ Fear of death decreases; ↑ Belief in afterlife/continuation increases

### **DOMAIN 8: PSYCHIC & EXPANDED PERCEPTION (PE)**

Definition: The emergence, enhancement, or awareness of experiences typically classified as psychic, paranormal, or anomalous—including heightened intuition, precognition, telepathy, clairvoyance, healing abilities, mediumistic experiences, ongoing out-of-body experiences, increased synchronicities, and electromagnetic (EM) sensitivity.

Literature Basis: Ring (1984) documented this extensively, developing a Psychic Experiences Inventory. Greyson has published multiple studies on paranormal aftereffects. Atwater (1988, 2007\) was particularly detailed about EM sensitivity (electronics malfunctioning, watches stopping, streetlights responding). Sutherland (1992) confirmed these patterns in Australian NDErs. These experiences are among the most challenging for NDErs because they are often socially stigmatized and difficult to disclose. The literature distinguishes between:

* Intuitive/precognitive: Knowing things before they happen, gut feelings that prove accurate  
* Telepathic/empathic: Sensing or hearing others' thoughts and feelings  
* Healing: Ability to channel healing energy through touch or intention  
* Mediumistic: Communication with deceased individuals  
* Clairvoyant/clairaudient: Seeing or hearing things not physically present  
* Out-of-body: Ongoing OBE capacity  
* Electromagnetic: Sensitivity to or interference with electronic devices  
* Synchronicity: Dramatic increases in meaningful coincidences

What to Look For in the Account:

* Reports of knowing things before they happen  
* Sensing others' emotions, thoughts, or physical pain  
* Experiences of healing others through touch, prayer, or intention  
* Communication with deceased loved ones after the NDE  
* Seeing auras, energy, or non-physical beings  
* Electronics malfunctioning in their presence  
* Watches stopping or batteries draining  
* Streetlights turning on or off  
* Dramatic increase in meaningful coincidences  
* Out-of-body experiences after the NDE  
* Dreams that prove prophetic  
* Development of any practice based on these abilities

Scoring Rubric:

| Score | Label | Description |
| ----- | ----- | ----- |
| 0 | Not Addressed | No mention of psychic or anomalous experiences |
| 1 | Briefly Noted | A single passing mention of heightened intuition or one anomalous event |
| 2 | Mild Change | A few psychic or anomalous experiences described |
| 3 | Moderate Change | Multiple psychic experiences described clearly; recognized as a pattern |
| 4 | Significant Change | Psychic experiences described as a regular part of life; may have developed practices around them |
| 5 | Profound Transformation | Psychic/expanded perception described as a defining feature of post-NDE life; multiple types reported; may have become a healer, medium, or intuitive practitioner |

Typical Direction: ↑ (New emergence or significant increase)

Subcategories (for detailed display):

* Intuition/Precognition  
* Empathic/Telepathic Sensitivity  
* Healing Abilities  
* Mediumistic Experiences  
* Electromagnetic Sensitivity  
* Other Anomalous Perception

### **DOMAIN 9: RELATIONSHIPS & SOCIAL DYNAMICS (RS)**

Definition: Changes in the experiencer's interpersonal relationships—including intimate partnerships, family relationships, friendships, and broader social dynamics—as well as the social challenges of being an NDEr.

Literature Basis: Relationship changes are among the most complex and often most painful aftereffects. Research consistently documents both deepening of some relationships and loss of others (Ring, 1984; Sutherland, 1992; Atwater, 2007; Greyson, 2021). Elevated divorce rates among NDErs have been noted in multiple studies. Common patterns include:

* The experiencer changes but their partner/family has not, creating a growing gulf  
* Difficulty tolerating superficial conversation or inauthentic relationships  
* Increased need for deep, meaningful connection  
* Feeling misunderstood, dismissed, or pathologized by family, friends, or clinicians  
* Difficulty explaining the experience to others  
* Social isolation or alienation  
* Formation of new relationships with like-minded people  
* Deeper, more authentic connections with some people  
* Increased boundary-setting (leaving toxic relationships)

This domain captures both the growth AND the struggle in relationships, making it important for those seeking to understand the full picture of NDE integration.

What to Look For in the Account:

* Changes in marriage or partnership (strengthened, strained, ended)  
* Changes in friendships (lost friends, gained new ones)  
* Family reactions to the experiencer's changes  
* Feeling misunderstood or unable to share the experience  
* Social isolation, loneliness, or alienation  
* New relationships with other NDErs or spiritual seekers  
* Difficulty with small talk or superficiality  
* Increased desire for deep, authentic connection  
* Setting boundaries or leaving unhealthy relationships  
* Changed parenting approach  
* Reactions from religious community, colleagues, medical professionals

Scoring Rubric:

| Score | Label | Description |
| ----- | ----- | ----- |
| 0 | Not Addressed | No mention of relationship changes |
| 1 | Briefly Noted | A passing mention of relationship shifts |
| 2 | Mild Change | Some description of changed relationships |
| 3 | Moderate Change | Clear description of meaningful relationship changes, with examples |
| 4 | Significant Change | Detailed account of major relationship restructuring (divorce, estrangement, new community, etc.) |
| 5 | Profound Transformation | Relationships described as fundamentally reconfigured; multiple relationships affected; detailed account of social upheaval and/or deepening |

Direction Indicator:

* ↑ Relationships deepened/improved overall  
* ↓ Relationships suffered/lost overall  
* ↕ Mixed—some deepened, some lost (most common pattern)  
* → Fundamentally restructured social world

---

### **DOMAIN 10: PURPOSE, MEANING & LIFE DIRECTION (PD)**

Definition: Changes in the experiencer's sense of life purpose, mission, calling, career direction, and quest for knowledge and understanding.

Literature Basis: A heightened sense of purpose or mission is widely documented (Ring, 1984; van Lommel, 2010; Greyson LCI-R "Quest for Meaning" factor; Atwater, 2007; Long/NDERF). Many NDErs report being told during their experience that they have a purpose or must return for a reason. Career changes—often away from high-earning positions and toward helping professions (nursing, counseling, social work, ministry, healing arts)—are frequently reported. An intense thirst for knowledge, especially about consciousness, spirituality, science, and philosophy, is common. Some NDErs report receiving specific instructions or a sense of mission during their experience.

What to Look For in the Account:

* Statements about having a purpose or mission  
* Being told to "go back" because they have something to do  
* Career changes (especially toward helping professions)  
* Leaving a lucrative career for meaningful work  
* Increased thirst for knowledge and learning  
* New educational pursuits  
* Interest in writing, speaking, or sharing their experience  
* Starting organizations, foundations, or initiatives  
* Becoming a teacher, counselor, healer, or advocate  
* Greater creativity or artistic expression  
* Quest for deeper understanding of life, consciousness, or reality  
* Feeling restless or unfulfilled in pre-NDE career  
* Describing a specific sense of what they're "supposed to do"

Scoring Rubric:

| Score | Label | Description |
| ----- | ----- | ----- |
| 0 | Not Addressed | No mention of changed purpose, meaning, or life direction |
| 1 | Briefly Noted | A passing mention of feeling a greater sense of purpose |
| 2 | Mild Change | Some description of shifted life direction or increased meaning-seeking |
| 3 | Moderate Change | Clear description of meaningful changes in purpose or direction, with examples |
| 4 | Significant Change | Detailed account of major life-direction change (career shift, educational pursuit, new calling) |
| 5 | Profound Transformation | Entire life reorganized around a new purpose or mission; dramatic career change; described as being given a specific mission; purpose is central to post-NDE identity |

Typical Direction: ↑ Sense of purpose increases; life direction often shifts toward service, knowledge, or creative expression

## **PART IV: META-SCORES & OVERALL SCORING**

### **4.1 Overall Transformation Score (OTS)**

Calculation: Sum of all 10 domain scores.

Range: 0–50

Interpretation:

| Score Range | Label | Description |
| ----- | ----- | ----- |
| 0 | No Transformation Discussed | The account focuses on the NDE itself without describing aftereffects |
| 1–10 | Minimal Transformation Noted | A few areas of change are briefly mentioned |
| 11–20 | Moderate Transformation | Several areas of meaningful change described |
| 21–30 | Significant Transformation | Multiple areas of clear, substantial transformation described |
| 31–40 | Major Transformation | Extensive, deep transformation across many life areas |
| 41–50 | Comprehensive Profound Transformation | Near-total life transformation described in vivid detail across virtually all domains |

Important Caveat for Display: A low score does NOT mean the experiencer was not transformed—it may mean they did not discuss transformation in this particular account. The scale measures *what is described*, not *what occurred*. This should be noted wherever the score is displayed:

*"This score reflects transformation as described in this account. A lower score may indicate the experiencer focused on describing the NDE itself rather than its aftereffects, not that transformation did not occur."*

### **4.2 Transformation Breadth (TB)**

Calculation: Count of domains scoring ≥1.

Range: 0–10

Interpretation: How many different areas of life were affected. Higher breadth \= more wide-ranging transformation.

| Breadth | Label |
| ----- | ----- |
| 0 | No transformation discussed |
| 1–3 | Focused transformation (few areas) |
| 4–6 | Moderate breadth |
| 7–8 | Broad transformation |
| 9–10 | Comprehensive transformation (nearly all life areas affected) |

### **4.3 Transformation Depth (TD)**

Calculation: Mean score of all domains scoring ≥1 (excludes domains scored 0).

Range: 1.0–5.0

Interpretation: How intensely transformation is described in the areas that were discussed. Higher depth \= more profound changes described.

| Depth | Label |
| ----- | ----- |
| 1.0–1.9 | Lightly described |
| 2.0–2.9 | Moderately described |
| 3.0–3.9 | Strongly described |
| 4.0–5.0 | Profoundly described |

### **4.4 Transformation Profile**

Beyond the numerical scores, the Transformation Profile is a qualitative summary consisting of:

1. Dominant Domains: The 2–3 highest-scoring domains (the areas of most prominent transformation)  
2. Direction Summary: A brief statement of the overall direction of change (e.g., "Shift from materialism and organized religion toward spirituality, compassion, and purpose-driven life")  
3. Integration Notes: Observations about how the experiencer describes their adjustment process—easy or difficult, supported or isolated, immediate or gradual, ongoing or resolved  
4. Transformation Timeline: Whether changes are described as immediate, gradual, or evolving over years/decades  
5. Notable Features: Any unique or distinctive aspects of this person's transformation (e.g., "Developed healing practice," "Founded charitable organization," "Became a hospice volunteer")

## **PART V: SPECIAL CONSIDERATIONS**

### **5.1 Distressing NDEs**

Accounts of distressing or frightening NDEs (Bush, 2012; Greyson & Bush, 1992\) may show distinctive transformation patterns:

* Initial increase in fear and anxiety before eventual transformation  
* Dramatic religious conversion in some cases  
* Profound behavior change (e.g., Howard Storm's account)  
* Transformation may take longer to manifest  
* The transformation may be as deep or deeper than from pleasurable NDEs

The AI should note when the NDE was distressing, as this contextualizes the transformation pattern.

### **5.2 Childhood NDEs**

Atwater's research shows that children who have NDEs may not recognize their transformation until adulthood. Their accounts may describe:

* Growing up feeling "different" without knowing why  
* Psychic abilities assumed to be normal (they didn't know others didn't have them)  
* Difficulty fitting in socially  
* Discovering the NDE framework later in life and recognizing their experiences

### **5.3 Account Length and Detail**

Longer, more detailed accounts will naturally score higher because there is more opportunity for transformation to be discussed. This is not a flaw—it reflects the richness of the data. However, the Transformation Depth score (mean of non-zero domains) helps normalize for this, as a short account that deeply describes one area of transformation will have a high Depth score even with a lower Overall score.

### **5.4 Cultural Context**

Transformation expressions may vary across cultures and religious traditions. An experiencer from a Hindu background may describe spiritual changes differently than one from a Christian background. The AI should assess transformation based on the *degree of change from the person's own baseline*, not against a universal template.

### **5.5 Temporal Dimension**

When discernible from the account, the AI should note:

* Time Since NDE: How long ago the NDE occurred (relevant because van Lommel's research shows transformation deepens over time)  
* Transformation Timeline: Immediate vs. gradual vs. delayed  
* Current Status: Whether transformation is described as ongoing, stable, or still evolving

The following prompt template is designed to be provided to an AI system along with a transcript or written account. It should produce consistent, reliable scoring.

You are an academic researcher specializing in near-death experience (NDE) aftereffects and transformation. You will analyze the following first-person NDE account and score it using the NDE Transformation Index (NDE-TI), a 10-domain scale measuring the transformation described by the experiencer as resulting from their NDE.

IMPORTANT INSTRUCTIONS:

1. Score ONLY what is described or clearly implied in the account. Do not infer or assume changes that are not mentioned.  
2. A score of 0 means the domain was NOT DISCUSSED in this account—it does NOT mean no change occurred.  
3. Assess the DEGREE OF CHANGE from the person's own described baseline, not against an external standard.  
4. Capture the DIRECTION of change using the indicators: ↑ (increased), ↓ (decreased), ↕ (mixed/complex), → (shifted/redirected), ✦ (newly emerged).
